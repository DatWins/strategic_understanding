White has a rook on a4 and f3-f4 pawn structure. Black has a rook on b3 and e6/f5 pawns. White to move — how does White fight for a draw?

A Plan A — Advance f5 to disrupt Black
Push f4-f5+ to check the king and disrupt Black's pawn structure.
B Plan B — Attack the e6 pawn with Ra6
Play Ra6 to attack the e6 pawn from behind and create counterplay.
C Plan C — Activate the king toward the center
Move the king to f4 to centralise and fight for the endgame.
✓ Correct — Plan B is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan B.

Why Plan B?

Ra6 attacks the e6 pawn from the flank, forcing Black's rook to defend it. This creates counterplay and prevents Black from advancing freely. Active rook play is essential when defending — the rook should attack, not sit passively.

Plan A (f5+) looks forcing but after …exf5 gxf5+ …Kxf5 White's pawns are broken and Black's active pieces win.

Plan C (Kf4) centralises the king but allows Black's rook to continue active operations on the b-file. Ra6 is more forcing.

Lesson: In rook endgames, activity is everything. An active rook attacking enemy pawns creates counterplay even in losing positions.
