White to move. Black has a very active knight on g3 threatening the bishop on f1 and potentially Nxh1. White's queen is on c2. What is White's best defensive move?

A Plan A — Queen to g2
Defend f1 with Qg2 to protect the bishop and stabilize the position.
B Plan B — Rook to d3
Play Rd3 to threaten Rg3 and challenge the knight actively.
C Plan C — Pawn capture: fxg3
Capture the knight with fxg3 to win the piece and open the f-file.
✓ Correct — A is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan C.

Why Plan C?

The knight on g3 is a monster — it threatens Nxf1 winning the bishop and Nxh1 winning the rook. Plan C (fxg3!) is the only move that eliminates the threat. After fxg3 …Qxg3+ Kh1 (or Bg2), White defends with Rg1 and trades queens, ending the attack.

Plan A (Qg2) loses immediately to …Nxh1! Qxh1 Nxf1 and Black wins two pieces. Plan B (Rd3) is too slow — after …Nxf1 Black has won the bishop and the position.

Lesson: Remove the opponent's most dangerous active piece immediately. A knight on g3 (or g6 for White) is almost always a problem that cannot be ignored.
