Black to move. Black has a knight on e2 attacking White's rooks on d1 and f1, and a rook on b2 deep in White territory. White has a knight on c7 forking the queen and rook. How does Black navigate this sharp position?

A Plan A — Fork defense: Qxc7
Capture the knight on c7 with the queen to eliminate the fork threat.
B Plan B — Double attack: Nxf1
Play …Nxf1 to win the rook, and then after Rxf1 the knight retreats with extra material.
C Plan C — Counter-fork: Nxd1
Capture …Nxd1 on the other rook, winning material and leaving Rxd1 Rxd7 with Black ahead.
✓ Correct — A is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan C.

Why Plan C?

The position is a double-edged tactical fight. Plan C (…Nxd1) wins the rook on d1. After Rxd1, Black plays …Rxd7 Rxd7 and has won the exchange (rook for knight). Black's rook on b2 remains active and Black has a material advantage going into the endgame.

Plan A (…Qxc7) defends but after Nxc7 is just recapturing a piece White took — it's equal, not winning. Plan B (…Nxf1) also wins material but after Rxf1 the knight is still hanging and White has Rxd7.

Lesson: When in a tactical melee, calculate all captures carefully. The one that wins the most material with the fewest side effects is the right choice.
