White has a passed pawn on c5 supported by the bishop pair. Black's knight on b6 eyes c4 and challenges the advanced pawn. How does White best exploit the c5 outpost?

A Plan A — Reinforce c5 with b4
Play b4 to firmly support the c5 pawn and prevent Black's …Nc4, creating a permanent space advantage.
B Plan B — Trade with cxb6
Capture cxb6 to open the c-file for the rook on b1 and double Black's pawns.
C Plan C — Knight to e4
Jump the knight to e4 to challenge Black's central control and support the c5 outpost from another angle.
✓ Correct — A is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan A.

Why Plan A?

The c5 pawn is a powerful outpost but needs reinforcement. Plan A (b4) is the right move: it stops …Nc4 permanently, secures c5, and begins a queenside advance (b4–b5 next) that restricts Black's knight on b6 to a bad square.

Plan B (cxb6) relieves the tension and gives Black's bishops room to breathe — after …axb6 Black gets open a- and c-files for the rooks with counterplay. Plan C (Ne4) is fine but not as forceful; it does not address the immediate threat to c5 and allows …Nc4.

Lesson: A space advantage must be maintained. Reinforce your advanced pawns before expanding further.
