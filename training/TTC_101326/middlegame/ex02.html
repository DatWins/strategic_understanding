White to move. The knight is already on f6 and the queen on f4. Black's king is on g7. White has a forcing combination here. Can you find the winning plan?

A Plan A — Dominate with Rd6
Swing the rook to d6 to pressure the queen on b6 and set up a back-rank mate.
B Plan B — Queen check: Qh6+
Play Qh6+ forcing Kf7 and then build the attack with Rg4.
C Plan C — Knight retreat and regroup
Retreat the knight to e4 to regroup and prepare a discovered attack.
✓ Correct — A is the best plan.
✗ Not quite. The answer is Plan B.

Why Plan B?

Plan B (Qh6+!) is the forcing move. After Qh6+ Kf7 (forced, since the king can't go to f8 due to Nxh7+ winning the queen), White plays Rg4! threatening Rg7#. The black queen cannot both defend g7 and stay active — White wins decisively.

Plan A (Rd6) is logical but slow — Black plays …Qb4 attacking the rook and creates counterplay. Plan C (retreating the knight) throws away the initiative; the knight on f6 is already in a winning position.

Lesson: When you have a check that disrupts the king's defense, play it and look for the follow-up before the king gets organized.
